3. Data We Process

What we process depends on how you use Taskel:

  • If you use Taskel without signing in (“guest” mode): a random identifier (a UUID), generated on your device, used only to label the data stored on that device. We do not collect your name, email address, or any other personal information in this mode.
  • If you sign in with Apple: Apple issues Taskel a stable, opaque identifier for your Apple ID’s relationship with this app; that identifier is what we store. Taskel’s sign-in request also asks Apple for permission to share your name and email address, as part of Apple’s standard sign-in flow, and if you choose “Hide My Email,” Apple may forward a private relay email address as part of that exchange. Taskel’s app code does not read or store your name or email address — real or private-relay — only the opaque identifier above is kept. How Apple itself handles the values it offers during sign-in is covered by Apple’s own Privacy Policy (linked in Section 8).
  • If you sign in with Google: the Google Sign-In SDK returns, and Taskel stores, a stable user identifier for your Google Account’s relationship with this app. Per Google’s own disclosure for this SDK, it also processes your device’s IP address, which Google uses for coarse, fraud-prevention location estimation (for example, flagging sign-in attempts from an unexpected location) — this happens between your device and Google directly, not through any server we operate.
  • Your habit, routine, task, and execution data: stored only on your device — see Section 4. We do not receive it.
  • Once advertising is added (not yet implemented — see Section 6), the advertising SDK will introduce additional data categories, such as device/advertising identifiers, coarse IP-derived location, and ad-interaction data. This section will be revised to reflect the actual configuration before that version ships.

4. Local-Only Habit and Routine Data

Everything you create in Taskel — routines, tasks, execution history, and statistics — is stored locally on your device using Apple’s SwiftData framework. We do not operate a server that receives, stores, or backs up this data; it is not synced or uploaded anywhere. Taskel’s own code makes no network requests to transmit it (we verified this by confirming the app contains no use of URLSession, the standard iOS networking API).

The one exception to “nothing leaves the device” is a third-party SDK’s own communication — specifically, the sign-in SDKs described in Section 3, and, once implemented, an advertising SDK (Section 6). Those SDKs communicate directly with their provider’s own servers, under that provider’s own privacy practices; none of that traffic passes through, or is logged by, any server we operate.

5. How We Use Data

We use the information described in Section 3 only to:

  • let you use the same sign-in across sessions on this device;
  • attribute your locally-stored data to your sign-in identifier instead of a random guest identifier, once you sign in;
  • help prevent fraudulent or abusive sign-in activity (this is what Google’s coarse, IP-based location estimate in Section 3 is for); and
  • once advertising is implemented, select and measure the ads shown to you (Section 6).

We do not use your information for any other purpose — for example, profiling unrelated to the above, or selling it (Section 8).

6. Advertising

Taskel does not currently show advertising, and no advertising SDK is included in the app as of this policy’s effective date.

We plan to introduce advertising in a future version, expected to use {{TODO:広告SDK}}. Before that version ships, this section (and Taskel’s App Store “App Privacy” disclosure) will be rewritten to match the actual SDK configuration. Our current working plan is:

  • Start with contextual / non-personalized ads, not ads personalized to you individually, and avoid collecting your device’s advertising identifier (IDFA) where the ad configuration allows it.
  • App Tracking Transparency (ATT): Apple requires an ATT permission prompt if the advertising in Taskel collects your device’s advertising identifier, links your device/account identifiers with data from other companies to serve or measure targeted ads, shares identifiers with an ad network in a way that enables retargeting, or performs cross-app or cross-site ad measurement. Including an ad SDK without showing an ATT prompt does not, by itself, satisfy this requirement — whether ATT applies depends on the ad SDK’s actual configuration and network behavior, which we will verify against Apple’s and Google’s documentation before release rather than assume.
  • Consent for EEA / UK / Switzerland: if Taskel serves personalized advertising to users in the EEA, UK, or Switzerland, Google requires use of a Google-certified Consent Management Platform (CMP) integrated with the IAB Transparency & Consent Framework (TCF) — in practice, Google’s User Messaging Platform (UMP) SDK — to obtain consent before any ad request. ATT and this consent mechanism address different legal requirements; neither substitutes for the other.
  • Data types the advertising SDK may process, once implemented, can include: device/advertising identifiers, technical/network information such as IP address, and information about ads shown to and interacted with. The exact set depends on the SDK version, ad format, and any mediation partners in use at the time.
  • If Taskel is ever placed in an audience or category directed at children under 13, personalized advertising will not be used for that audience, consistent with Section 12.

Using a third-party advertising SDK does not reduce Taskel’s responsibility for what that SDK collects and how it’s used — including declaring it accurately here and in the App Store’s App Privacy labels, and obtaining any permission or consent (ATT, CMP) the SDK’s actual behavior requires. “The SDK collected it, not our own code” is not treated as an exemption.

7. Legal Bases for Processing (EEA / UK Users)

For users in the EEA or UK, we process the data described in Section 3 on the following legal bases under the GDPR / UK GDPR:

  • Authentication (Sign in with Apple / Google): performance of a contract with you — providing the sign-in feature you chose to use — and, for the fraud-prevention aspect of Google’s coarse location check, our legitimate interest in keeping sign-in secure, balanced against your rights.
  • Advertising, once implemented: we intend to rely on your consent for personalized advertising and related measurement shown to EEA/UK users, obtained through the CMP described in Section 6, rather than on legitimate interest. You can withdraw that consent at any time (Section 11) once those in-app controls exist.

8. How We Share Data

We do not sell your personal information, and we do not share it with third parties for their own independent marketing purposes. The only recipients of the limited data described in Section 3 are:

  • Apple and Google, as providers of the optional sign-in features you choose to use — see their own privacy policies: apple.com/legal/privacy and policies.google.com/privacy
  • Once advertising is implemented (Section 6): our advertising SDK provider(s), together with any mediation partners they use, for the purpose of serving and measuring ads; and
  • anyone we’re required to disclose information to by law, or to protect Taskel’s, our users’, or others’ rights, safety, or property.

We do not otherwise share your data with third parties. If advertising-related sharing is later found to meet the definition of a “sale” or “share” of personal information under a law such as the CCPA/CPRA, we will update this section and provide any required opt-out before that applies.

9. International Data Transfers

Because Taskel does not operate a server that receives your habit/routine or account data, we do not ourselves transfer that data internationally. Where you sign in, or once advertising is implemented, the identifiers and technical data described in Section 3 may be processed by Apple, Google, or an advertising partner outside your country of residence, under their own privacy policies and any safeguards they apply to international transfers (for example, GDPR Chapter V mechanisms, where relevant).

10. Retention and Deletion

  • Local habit/routine/task data and your sign-in identifier are retained only on your device, for as long as you keep them there. You can remove them using Taskel’s in-app deletion control, or by uninstalling the app — see Section 11 for details.
  • Sign-in and, once implemented, advertising SDK data held by Apple, Google, or an ad partner follows their own retention policies; we do not control it directly. Section 11 explains your options for managing it.
  • We do not retain any copy of your data beyond the above, because we never receive it in the first place.
  • Nothing here overrides retention we may be legally required to observe (for example, in response to a valid legal request); we are not currently aware of any such obligation applying to Taskel.

11. Your Rights and Choices

Depending on where you live, you may have rights under laws such as Japan’s APPI, the EU/UK GDPR, the California CCPA/CPRA, or Brazil’s LGPD — for example, to access, correct, delete, or export your personal information, to object to or restrict certain processing, to withdraw consent, and to lodge a complaint with your local data protection authority.

In practice, because Taskel does not store your habit/routine or account data on any server:

  • Access / correction / export: your data is visible and editable directly in the app at all times; there is nothing held elsewhere for us to act on separately.
  • Deletion: Apple requires apps that offer account creation to let users start deletion from inside the app, so we treat an in-app “Delete Account” control in Settings as required, not optional (not yet available as of this policy’s effective date). Once available, it will permanently remove your locally-stored sign-in identifier and all locally-stored routines, tasks, and execution history, and return the app to its first-launch state; this cannot be undone. Until it ships, uninstalling the app achieves the same result — because nothing is sent to us, there is nothing left on our side to delete separately.
  • Sign-in management: you can stop using Sign in with Apple/Google for Taskel, and review what those providers know about the connection, through your Apple ID or Google Account settings at any time.
  • Advertising choices, once implemented: you will be able to withdraw advertising consent and manage ad personalization through in-app controls described in Section 6.
  • For anything not covered above — including a formal data-subject request or a complaint to your local supervisory authority — contact us using Section 15.

13. Security

We take reasonable, common-sense measures appropriate to an app that keeps your data on your own device rather than on a server we operate: your habit/routine data is never transmitted, and your sign-in identifier is stored using standard iOS app-data storage. No method of storage or transmission is completely secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security — the same is true of any app or service. Your device’s own security (passcode, encryption, and so on) is an important part of protecting the data Taskel stores on it.
